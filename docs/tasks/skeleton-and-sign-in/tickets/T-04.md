## What to build

Design section 9.2 in full: the per-address rate limit (step 1, with the
`sign_in_attempts` table as a new migration and the client-address rule at the
end of 9.2), failure counting, the 15-minute lock, the reset rules and the
`ACCOUNT_LOCKOUT` audit row by SYSTEM (steps 4 to 6).

`tests/implemented_rules.txt` gains IAM-R04, IAM-R05, IAM-R06 and IAM-R07.

## Behaviours to prove

Spec scenarios
1. Every scenario of rules IAM-R04 to IAM-R07 runs and passes: 7 scenarios,
   10 cases, including the lock boundary (14 min 59 s LOCKED, 15 min 00 s
   signed in) and the count resets, all driven by the test clock.
2. The `@concurrency` scenario of IAM-R07 uses truly simultaneous connections,
   and with the guard removed it fails every time over repeated runs. Remove
   the guard in a throwaway copy, run it at least 20 times, and report the
   count in the Ready comment.

Details the scenarios do not pin down
3. An attempt refused as RATE_LIMITED is not itself recorded, shows
   「登入嘗試過於頻繁，請稍後再試」, and does not touch any account's failure
   count.
4. An attempt during a lock does not verify the password, does not add to the
   failure count and does not extend the lock.
5. The failure count survives the refusal: it is committed even though the
   request is refused (the one deliberate exception in design 9.2).
6. Two wrong passwords arriving at the same instant for an account at 4
   failures produce one lock and one `ACCOUNT_LOCKOUT` row.
7. With `TRUSTED_PROXY_HOPS=0` the address is the connection's peer and
   `X-Forwarded-For` is ignored; with 1 it is the right-most forwarded value;
   a value the client put further left is never used.
8. Failures for an unknown or archived name change no account and still count
   toward the address's rate limit.

Carried over from the T-02 verdict (pull request 9, findings 1 and 3)
9. A sign-in whose name contains a NUL byte is refused as INVALID with
   「請選擇帳戶」 and writes nothing (today it is a 500 from the database).
   The same holds for any other text field the sign-in form sends.
10. The list of sign-in groups has one owner: adding a group in one place
    cannot leave the picker offering a group the server does not know. Today
    it is written in both `app/accounts/store.py` and
    `app/accounts/service.py`.
